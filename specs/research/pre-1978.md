# Research: extending the window back to 1936 (2026-09-28)

What it would take to start the window at the AP poll's debut instead of 1978, from the
product side and the data side. The numbers come from a throwaway simulation over Howell's
team pages (all 305 byconf teams, fetched 2026-09-28) and every AP poll on
collegepollarchive. It uses Howell's scores only, with no alt-home rulings and no vote, so
treat the streak lengths as close, not final.

## Decisions (Drew, 2026-09-28)

1. Streak holders stay current FBS teams. Former majors appear only as opponents.
2. A game played before the season's first AP poll has an unknown rank for both teams. Rank
   chips skip it, the same way betting chips skip unlined games.
3. "Ranked" means whatever the AP ranked that week: top 20 through 1960, top 10 in 1961–67,
   top 20 in 1968–88, top 25 from 1989. The chip's info text says so.
4. A third outcome mode, "unbeaten" (wins and ties), sits next to winning and losing.
5. The span bug gets fixed first (see below).

## There is no FBS before 1978

The NCAA had no divisions before 1956, a University Division and a College Division from
1956 to 1972, and Division I from 1973 until the I-A/I-AA split in 1978. Howell's
`byconf.htm` already covers this. It lists the teams he considers "FBS or the equivalent"
for every season back to 1869, and `parse-jhowell.mjs` already reads it for `teams[].fbs`.
So "FBS" just becomes "major that season." His team pages list only major seasons: none of
the 245 pages with 1936+ seasons has a season outside his spans.

| season | majors | current FBS among them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1936 | 131 | 75 |
| 1950 | 120 | 80 |
| 1960 | 113 | 83 |
| 1970 | 123 | 97 |
| 1977 | 145 | 106 |
| 1982 | 113 | 102 |

221 teams were major at some point in 1936–77, and 112 of them aren't FBS now (Ivies,
Fordham, Duquesne, Santa Clara, Chicago, Great Lakes Navy, Iowa Pre-Flight). They barely
reach the boards. The best former-major winning streak is Princeton's 24 (1949–52), and the
plain winning top 12 cuts off at 25. The best losing streak is Hardin-Simmons' 27 (1959–62),
which would tie for 4th. Giving them rows would cost logos, team-info and aliases for 112
teams, several with no ESPN id, and the active scope means nothing for them. That's why
decision 1.

## What changes on the boards

- The plain all-time winning board gets Oklahoma's 47 (1953–57) at #1, then Toledo 35
  (1969–71), Texas 30 (1968–70), Michigan State 28 (1950–53), Army 25 (1944–46) and
  Michigan 25 (1946–49).
- The default board (vs unranked) doesn't change at the top. Alabama's 100 stays #1, and
  the best pre-1978 run is Oklahoma's 37 at about #11.
- Kansas State's 28 (1945–48) and Virginia's 28 (1958–60) join the losing board behind
  Northwestern's 34.
- Ties are 3.7% of pre-1978 team-games. With ties breaking streaks, Notre Dame's 39
  unbeaten (1946–50) and Army's 32 (1944–47) vanish. Unbeaten mode brings them back
  (decision 4). Under it, Oklahoma's run is 48.

## Games against non-majors

Today a loss to an FCS team breaks a streak and a win over one counts, same as any other
opponent (spec, Amendments: FCS methodology). The planned "vs FBS opponents" chip is where
FCS wins would stop counting. The question was whether pre-1978 needs a different rule. It
doesn't. Non-major opponents are 12.7% of current-FBS team-games in 1936–45 (service teams
and small colleges during the war), 4.9% in 1946–77, 4.1% in 1978–2005 and 6.4% in
2006–25. If wins over non-majors were skipped, the well-known streaks change in every era,
not just the early ones:

| streak | counting every game | skipping non-major wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alabama vs unranked, 2007–21 | 100 | 88 (12 FCS wins) |
| Miami, 2000–02 | 34 | 33 |
| Army, 1944–46 | 25 | 23 |
| Tennessee, 1937–39 | 23 | out of the top 10 |

Published streaks (the NCAA record book, the press) count every game. So one rule works in
every era: count every game. The "vs FBS opponents" chip becomes "vs major opponents," and
membership comes from Howell's spans in every era, so no era-specific rule is needed. Howell
counts some wartime service teams (Great Lakes Navy, Iowa Pre-Flight, Randolph Field) as
majors, and that chip would inherit his call.

## Rank semantics

- The first poll of each season lands in mid-October from 1936 to 1949. The preseason poll
  starts in 1950. 12,858 of 48,627 pre-1978 team-games have no poll in effect. Today
  `rankOf` returns 0 for those, which reads as unranked. Decision 2 needs a third value.
- Poll depth by era: 20 (1936–60), 10 (1961–67), 20 (1968–88), 25 (1989+). The top-10 and
  top-5 chips mean the same thing in every era. "Ranked" and "unranked" don't (decision 3).
- Pre-1968 final polls were released before the bowls (1965 excepted). collegepollarchive
  headings have no date for final polls, and `parsePollPage` dates them Feb 1 of the next
  year. For these seasons a final poll should be dated just after the last regular-season
  poll, so bowls use it.

## Chip floors

The betting chips floor at 1978. No bulk source of closing lines before Repole is known.
Night (2002), halftime and overtime (2001) and possession (2004) keep their floors. Site,
rank, conference, coach, calendar, context, game shape and rivalry work back to 1936.

Stopping at 1936 is a product call, not a data limit. Howell goes back to 1869, and only
the rank chips need the AP poll. Before 1936, though, only 75% of his rows are verified and
fewer of the majors are current FBS.

## Code that assumes 1978

- `src/lib/model.js` packs per-season ordinal keys as `se - 1978` in 64 slots.
- `fbsStartOf` falls back to 1978. The copy in `TeamPanel.jsx`, `definition.js` (betting
  note), `gen-og.mjs` and `prerender.mjs` says 1978.
- `parse-jhowell.mjs` and `parse-coaches.mjs` filter to 1978+. `build-payload.mjs` joins
  `spine-1978-2013.json`.
- Crown mining (~4s) should scale with games per team, so expect ~7s.

## Bugs found along the way (both fixed 2026-09-28)

- **Spans break on seasons a team didn't play.** Howell ends a span on any missing season,
  and `fbsStartOf` takes the latest span. In the live site, UConn, New Mexico State and Old
  Dominion start at 2021 (they skipped 2020), SMU at 1989 (the death penalty) and UAB at
  2017 (the shutdown). A 1936 window would also cut about 35 teams at 1943–45, Alabama
  included. Fixed by `data/ref/fbs-span-bridges.json`. New Mexico State's 2020 season was
  two spring games no source lists (L 17–43 Tarleton State, W 36–29 Dixie State), now in
  `data/ref/extra-games.json`.
- **Miami's Howell pages never parsed.** The season header regex in `parse-jhowell.mjs`
  can't handle "2001-Miami (Florida) (Big East)". Both Miamis have no conference history
  before 2006 in the payload, and Howell's side of their games is missing from the vote.
  The regex now takes the last parenthetical as the conference. No game rows changed.
