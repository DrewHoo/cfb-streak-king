## (b) The pattern

Two kinds of indie source get cited:

- **A stable reference page with a permalink.** Winsipedia's matchup pages and Sports Reference fit here. Writers credit them in one line ("courtesy of", "according to") in game previews and series-history pieces (#9–14). Most of these writers work on team sites (SI FanNation, 247) that publish a preview every week.
- **A recurring weekly artifact that someone posts on X.** @CFB_Data's charts and Game on Paper's team tables fit here. These get embedded or hat-tipped in weekly rankings and betting columns (#4–6). The citing writers are the ones who produce a weekly format: Davis's previews, De Lima's rankings and best bets, Daughters' "things to know" and "streaks on the line".

Media pickups of Reddit work were one-off novelties: a curse, a map (#15–16). In the CBS example (#1) the data was supplied to the outlet directly, so pitching works there. I found no evidence of tip emails for the others; they appear to come from writers who follow the account. Game on Paper recruits podcasts openly ("TAG YOUR FAVORITE CFB PODCAST… our DM's are open"): https://x.com/gameonpaper/status/1952782025611505776

What gets cited is something checkable and dated, framed as a ranking or a record, at a URL the writer can link. Streak listicles are an evergreen format that outlets keep republishing, and they usually credit nobody (the uncredited list above). That points to a source writers can use and cite, which nobody fills today.

## (c) The gap

**ESPN.** Broadcast research is in-house. Each ABC crew has a dedicated researcher, with the work prepped months ahead and fed through an earpiece and an iPad research chat. ESPN's press piece names no outside tools: https://espnpressroom.com/feature/behind-the-broadcast-how-espn-sports-research-powers-espn-and-abcs-college-football-coverage/

**Elias.** Elias Sports Bureau is the stats source for ESPN and CBS (secondary source): https://www.proklamate.com/blog/the-wizardry-of-sports-stats-where-do-analysts-like-herbstreit-and-fowler-get-all-those-numbers

**ESPN Stats & Info.** A radio research column credits most of its items to "ESPN Stats & Info": https://arizonasports.com/uncategorized/researchdoug-football-season-is-here

**Stathead:**
- Stathead offers a Streak Finder for football in general: https://faq.sports-reference.com/portal/en/kb/articles/overview-of-stathead-search-tools
- Its college launches were a player season finder (2024-06) and a player game finder (2024-11), with a team game finder promised. No college streak tool is announced: https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/2024/06/college-football-stats-have-arrived-in-stathead/ and https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/2024/11/a-new-college-football-tool-launches-on-stathead/
- The Team Streak Finder appears listed only for pro football: https://stathead.com/football/team-streak-finder.cgi
- **This is uncertain.** The Stathead pages returned 403, so I could not confirm whether a college team streak mode exists.

**StatMuse.** No CFB citations found.

**Team SIDs' game notes.** No sourced evidence found either way.

**The gap.** Writers produce conditional streak lists (home, vs unranked, on the line this week) and cite no tool for them. The streaks they do credit come from series-only sources (Winsipedia) or general databases (Sports Reference). Nobody I found offers a weekly, linkable list of streaks under conditions or of streaks on the line. Daughters' annual "streaks on the line" piece is that idea done once a year by hand.
